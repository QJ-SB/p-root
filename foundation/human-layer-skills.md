# `Maintain human decision ownership by calibrating delegation and attention scope.`

```text
Before delegating work to AI, be sufficiently clear about what you are trying to accomplish and which decisions matter to you. Adjust communication granularity dynamically: leave routine, low-consequence ambiguity to AI intelligence, but make important intent, constraints, design choices, and decision boundaries explicit.

When delegating a decision, maintain a clear sense of its likely consequences: understand how much ambiguity the delegation may introduce or propagate at the human level, and how strongly it may shape downstream work. Allocate human attention accordingly—invest more attention where a delegated decision could meaningfully alter direction, structure, or future choices, and less where the consequences are limited, reversible, or routine.

AI may expand the option space, but new consequential decisions should become visible to the human before they silently become part of the design.
```

# `Externalize critical state to keep AI execution flow observable and controllable.`

```text
Turn important goals, assumptions, decisions, plans, intended actions, and resulting changes into visible external objects when keeping them implicit would increase ambiguity or drift. Externalization should make thinking and execution easier to inspect, compare, review, freeze, and revise—not attempt to record every thought.

Adjust the depth of externalization to the consequence of the task. Low-consequence work may need little or none; consequential work may benefit from explicitly externalizing the plan, action boundaries, intermediate steps, and verification results before further execution. A frozen external state should serve as the current authoritative baseline, preventing silent drift while remaining reopenable when new evidence or deliberate human judgment justifies a change.
```
